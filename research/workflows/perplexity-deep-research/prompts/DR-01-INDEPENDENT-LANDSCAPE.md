# DR-01: independent landscape

Copy everything below this line into Perplexity Deep Research. Run it independently from Comet's first result to reduce confirmation bias.

---

Today is 20 August 2026. Conduct deep research to discover evidence-backed problems in Indian public-service websites and digital services that could become projects for the Build What Moves India hackathon.

Do not ask broad clarifying questions. The scope and evaluation requirements are defined below. Use the general research workflow, perform several focused search rounds, open the underlying sources, cross-check material claims, and return one copyable Markdown report.

## Scope

- Cover central, state, and local public services in India.
- Study resident-facing journeys involving applications, claims, certificates, benefits, grievances, corrections, renewals, eligibility, appointments, document submission, status tracking, and escalation.
- Look for broken rules, handoffs, state transitions, responsibility gaps, unexplained rejections, correction loops, repeated data entry, inaccessible instructions, language barriers, and other process failures.
- Prioritize mobile users, slow connections, limited digital experience, rural users, older people, migrant workers, and assisted-service users.
- At least 14 of the 20 candidates must be outside EPFO and IRCTC because those services are already crowded in public discussion.
- Do not propose generic chatbots, dashboards, document summarizers, or interface redesigns unless the evidence identifies one exact failed journey and an underlying process problem.

## Safety

- Do not interact with, test, automate, crawl, scrape, log in to, or submit anything to a live government system.
- Do not use undocumented APIs.
- Use indexed public pages, public non-transactional documents, official reports, audits, parliamentary material, court records, published research, and reputable reporting.
- Do not collect or reproduce personal IDs, passwords, OTPs, payment information, claim numbers, health information, or other sensitive data.

## Evidence rules

- Tier A: official Indian rules, manuals, reports, audits, parliamentary material, court records, and public-authority documents.
- Tier B: peer-reviewed or method-based institutional research.
- Tier C: reputable reporting that names its evidence.
- Tier D: individual public accounts. Use them as discovery signals, not proof of prevalence.
- Tier E: commercial blogs, marketing, AI summaries, and copied articles. Use only as leads.
- Do not use search snippets as final evidence.
- Record source dates and check whether the problem is current, historical, resolved, regional, or unclear.
- Do not count several copies of one original report as independent confirmation.
- Do not infer prevalence from anecdotes.
- Separate facts, inference, recommendation, and unknowns.
- Do not mix this event with another hackathon or import rules from other competitions.
- Internet research cannot establish that our team faced a problem. Mark every candidate `TEAM CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`.

## Hackathon fit

A useful candidate must concern an Indian public-service digital journey, define one user and one start-to-finish problem, address more than visual design, permit a safe working prototype with synthetic data, avoid live government integration, and be narrow enough for three people to build by 27 August 2026.

Find 20 distinct candidate problems. Do not recommend a winner yet.
