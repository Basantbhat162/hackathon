## Research tasks

For each candidate:

- Find current official tools, redesigns, help systems, and announced fixes.
- Find exact or closely related private products, civic-tech tools, NGO projects, GitHub repositories, academic prototypes, and previous hackathon projects.
- Distinguish an exact solution to the same journey from a general chatbot, content site, or loosely related platform.
- Explain what is already solved and what remains unsolved according to evidence.
- Do not treat an absent search result as proof that no solution exists.
- Identify the smallest end-to-end prototype three people could build before 27 August 2026.
- List the required components, published rules, synthetic-data needs, major unknowns, and features that must be excluded.
- Define one measurable before-and-after demonstration.
- Define one judge-visible demo moment under 60 seconds.
- Assess product AI as `ESSENTIAL`, `USEFUL BUT OPTIONAL`, `UNNECESSARY`, or `UNPROVEN`. Do not force runtime AI into the product merely because Codex is mandatory during development.
- Explain why deterministic rules, search, or templates are insufficient if AI is claimed to be essential.
- Assess the harm of incorrect output and whether all user-facing rules have primary sources.
- Do not score the strategic rubric. The mandatory gate has not yet been cleared by web research.

## Restrictions

- Do not recommend live government integration, private APIs, scraping, real credentials, real personal data, OTPs, or payments.
- Do not download, clone, install, or run discovered projects.
- Do not import rules from other hackathons.
- Do not treat marketing claims, repository stars, or isolated comments as proof of impact.
