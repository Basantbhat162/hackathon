## Research coverage

**Search groups used** (3 rounds, 3 queries each, total 9 queries):
1. CAG/audit reports on digital public service delivery; CSC rural user issues; scholarship portal rejections.
2. CPGRAMS grievance redressal delays; land mutation portal errors; e-Shram migrant worker registration.
3. Birth/death certificate (CRS) delays; ration card e-KYC/portability; municipal property-tax portal issues.
4. EPS-95/pension grievance portals; UDID disability certificate delays; RTO/Parivahan Aadhaar-OTP mobile update issues.

**Date and language coverage:** Prioritized sources from Jan 2024–Aug 2026 (CAG Report No. 5/2026, DARPG CPGRAMS monthly reports 2025, RGI circular March 2025, IJFMR e-Shram study 2025). One 2017 Hindu Centre CSC report and 2022 ORGI CRS rule were checked against 2025–26 sources and found still cited/applicable. All sources retrieved were in English; no regional-language original sources were reviewed directly (a coverage limit). Instagram/YouTube/Facebook/LinkedIn posts were treated only as discovery signals, not proof of prevalence. Some sites (Karnataka Bhoomi mutation search) returned "no information available" without login — recorded as access barrier, not tested further. ### CB-C01 — PDS e-KYC mismatch blocks ration access
- **Service/user:** Public Distribution System (PDS) e-KYC verification; ration cardholders, disproportionately elderly and rural residents with fluctuating biometrics or address mismatches.
- **Precise journey:** Cardholder visits fair-price shop or portal to complete mandatory e-KYC → system checks Aadhaar demographic data against ration card database → mismatch triggers "field verification required" status.
- **Exact failure point:** No standard online path to correct or contest a mismatch; the field officer's decision and timeline are opaque to the beneficiary.
- **Underlying process problem:** The verification workflow defaults to manual, discretionary field checks with no defined SLA or appeal escalation captured in the digital record.
- **Why deeper than interface design:** The failure is a backend eligibility/verification rule (data-matching threshold plus offline adjudication), not a UI issue.
- **Documented effect:** Card can be blocked pending verification, risking loss of subsidised food access.
- **Prevalence status:** ESTIMATED BY SOURCE (based on DFPD circular design intent and ongoing 2026 state e-KYC campaigns).
- **Currency evidence:** e-KYC enforcement drives are actively running mid-2026.
- **Access/literacy dimension:** Elderly/rural users with biometric degradation are most affected; regional-language notices vary by state.
- **Safe prototype boundary:** Mock a synthetic ration-card/Aadhaar dataset with intentional mismatches; simulate a transparent verification-status and appeal tracker. No live PDS integration.
- **Before/after comparison:** Time from "mismatch flagged" to "beneficiary informed of exact reason and next step" — measurable in a prototype without invented statistics.
- **Lived-problem status:** TEAM CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM.
- **Unanswered questions:** National rejection/appeal-success rate not found in this search.

### CB-C02 — e-Shram registration barriers for informal/migrant workers
- **Service/user:** e-Shram portal (Ministry of Labour); informal and migrant workers, especially women.
- **Precise journey:** Worker attempts self-registration using Aadhaar + linked mobile + bank account → selects occupation from fixed list → submits.
- **Exact failure point:** Missing linked mobile number, no bank account, or occupation absent from dropdown causes abandonment or misclassification.
- **Underlying process problem:** Eligibility chain (ID + bank + mobile, all linked) assumes formal-sector documentation completeness.
- **Why deeper than interface design:** This is a structural eligibility/data-model problem — the occupation taxonomy and identity-linkage rules exclude real work categories, not a form-layout issue.
- **Documented effect:** Exclusion from social security registration database; benefits contingent on registration are inaccessible.
- **Prevalence status:** MEASURED (small-sample survey: 36% of 39 registered users faced problems; 47% of unregistered cited "didn't know where to register").
- **Currency evidence:** 30.97 crore registrations by July 2025, but structural gaps persist per 2025 policy review.
- **Access/literacy dimension:** Language limited largely to Hindi/English initially, low smartphone/data access among target population.
- **Safe prototype boundary:** Mock worker profiles with edge-case occupations/no-bank scenarios; demonstrate an inclusive occupation-taxonomy and offline-assist flow. No real Aadhaar/bank data.
- **Before/after comparison:** Number of synthetic "unclassifiable occupation" cases resolved by improved taxonomy vs. current dropdown.
- **Lived-problem status:** TEAM CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH.
- **Unanswered questions:** Current national-scale rejection rate not found in this search.

### CB-C03 — CSC overcharging for nominally free/fixed-price services
- **Service/user:** Common Service Centres (CSC/Digital Seva); rural citizens seeking Aadhaar or certificate services.
- **Precise journey:** Citizen visits CSC for Aadhaar update or certificate → pays VLE-quoted fee → no receipt or rate list shown.
- **Exact failure point:** Absence of displayed official rate list; VLEs charge "market rate" well above notified fees (e.g., Rs.50-100 vs. notified free/Rs.15).
- **Underlying process problem:** Weak grievance/rate-enforcement mechanism; guideline compliance not audited at point of service.
- **Why deeper than interface design:** This is a governance/enforcement gap (pricing transparency and grievance escalation), not a screen design issue.
- **Documented effect:** Citizens pay informally-set premiums for statutorily free/cheap services.
- **Prevalence status:** ANECDOTAL (qualitative 2017 study; issue still referenced in 2025 scheme evaluation citing "fragmentation" and "capacity gaps").
- **Currency evidence:** 2025 IMPRI review confirms structural issues persist, though doesn't re-quantify overcharging specifically.
- **Access/literacy dimension:** Rural, older, less digitally literate citizens most reliant on VLE intermediaries and least likely to know official rates.
- **Safe prototype boundary:** Mock a rate-transparency/receipt-verification tool citizens could use at any CSC with synthetic transaction data; no real CSC transaction scraping.
- **Before/after comparison:** Rate of synthetic "overcharge flagged vs. accepted silently" in prototype testing.
- **Lived-problem status:** TEAM CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.
- **Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM (evidence dated; currency inferred, not directly measured 2024-26).
- **Unanswered questions:** Current overcharging complaint volume not found in this search.

### CB-C04 — CPGRAMS "resolved" ≠ actually resolved
- **Service/user:** CPGRAMS (DARPG); any citizen filing a grievance against any department.
- **Precise journey:** Citizen files grievance → department marks it "resolved"/closed → citizen has no structured re-verification step.
- **Exact failure point:** Administrative closure recorded identically to substantive resolution in the system status field.
- **Underlying process problem:** No citizen-confirmation or satisfaction-verification gate before a grievance is marked closed.
- **Why deeper than interface design:** This is a workflow/state-machine problem in grievance lifecycle management, not a display issue.
- **Documented effect:** Citizens may believe issue addressed when it is not; erodes trust in redress mechanism.
- **Prevalence status:** ESTIMATED BY SOURCE ("~65% resolved within 30 days, but significant portion administrative closures").
- **Currency evidence:** Based on DARPG 2024-25 annual report as interpreted by a 2026 RTI-focused explainer.
- **Access/literacy dimension:** Disproportionately affects citizens without follow-up capacity (rural, low literacy) who don't re-file.
- **Safe prototype boundary:** Mock a grievance dataset with "closed" vs "citizen-confirmed resolved" states; build a satisfaction-confirmation loop demo. No live CPGRAMS submission.
- **Before/after comparison:** Fraction of synthetic grievances reopened after a confirmation step vs. none currently.
- **Lived-problem status:** TEAM CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM (single secondary source interpreting primary data; independent corroboration not found in this search).
- **Unanswered questions:** Original DARPG 2024-25 report's own framing of "administrative closures" not independently verified in this search.

### CB-C05 — CPGRAMS pendency/backlog at state level
- **Service/user:** CPGRAMS; grievants awaiting redress from state/UT departments.
- **Precise journey:** Grievance filed → enters queue → remains pending beyond expected cycle.
- **Exact failure point:** 1,88,408 grievances pending across States/UTs as of Jan 31, 2025; 67,859 pending centrally as of Nov 2025.
- **Underlying process problem:** Structural mismatch between monthly inflow and disposal capacity in specific ministries/states.
- **Why deeper than interface design:** Capacity/staffing and workflow-routing problem, not a portal UI issue.
- **Documented effect:** Citizens experience extended wait beyond the stated 21-30 day target.
- **Prevalence status:** MEASURED (official monthly DARPG figures).
- **Currency evidence:** Reports through November 2025.
- **Access/literacy dimension:** Rural/low-bandwidth users face added difficulty tracking status without smartphone/data access.
- **Safe prototype boundary:** Mock a grievance-routing simulator with synthetic ministry queues showing bottleneck visualization; no live pgportal access.
- **Before/after comparison:** Synthetic average pendency time before/after a proposed routing/prioritization change.
- **Lived-problem status:** TEAM CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.
- **Confidence:** HIGH (official measured data).
- **Unanswered questions:** State-by-state root cause (staffing vs. process) not found in this search.

### CB-C06 — Hospitals delay proactive birth/death registration
- **Service and affected user:** Civil Registration System (CRS), overseen by the Registrar General of India; affected users are families of newborns and deceased persons, especially those depending on hospitals to initiate registration. [crsorgi.gov](https://crsorgi.gov.in/)
- **One precise user journey:** A birth or death occurs in a hospital; the family expects the hospital or registrar to report it on the CRS portal; the certificate is not issued within the expected period, so the family must follow up manually. [sabrangindia](https://sabrangindia.in/rgi-warns-hospitals-over-delay-in-birth-and-death-reporting/)
- **Exact failure point:** The March 17, 2025 RGI circular says some hospitals were waiting for family members to initiate registration or directing them to do it themselves, instead of reporting the event promptly. [sabrangindia](https://sabrangindia.in/rgi-warns-hospitals-over-delay-in-birth-and-death-reporting/)
- **Underlying process problem:** The legal and operational responsibility sits with hospitals/registrars, but enforcement is weak enough that the default burden shifts back to families. [drishtiias](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/civil-registration-system-crs-report-2024)
- **Why this is deeper than interface design:** The portal can exist and still fail if the hospital-as-registrar process is not followed; this is an accountability and workflow problem, not just a screen-design issue. [sabrangindia](https://sabrangindia.in/rgi-warns-hospitals-over-delay-in-birth-and-death-reporting/)
- **Documented effect on the user:** Delayed certificates can affect school admissions, marriage registration, and government employment because the digital birth certificate became the sole official proof of date of birth from October 2023. [sabrangindia](https://sabrangindia.in/rgi-warns-hospitals-over-delay-in-birth-and-death-reporting/)
- **Prevalence status:** `MEASURED`. The RGI circular and CRS reporting described this as a systemic compliance issue, not an isolated case. [drishtiias](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/civil-registration-system-crs-report-2024)

- **Mobile, bandwidth, language, or digital-literacy issue:** Families with low digital confidence are least able to detect whether the hospital has actually registered the event. [sabrangindia](https://sabrangindia.in/rgi-warns-hospitals-over-delay-in-birth-and-death-reporting/)

- **Confidence:** `HIGH`.
- **Unanswered questions:** Post-circular compliance improvement rates by state were not found in this search.
