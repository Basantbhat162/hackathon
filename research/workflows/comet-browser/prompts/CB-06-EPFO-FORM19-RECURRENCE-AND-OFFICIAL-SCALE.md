## Official-source search order

Search and open the underlying documents in this order:

1. EPFO annual reports, dashboards, FAQs, manuals, circulars, Citizens' Charter, grievance material, office instructions, and published statistics.
2. Ministry of Labour and Employment annual reports, releases, dashboards, and parliamentary material.
3. Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha questions and annexures on EPFO claim rejection, return, pendency, settlement time, auto-settlement, grievance volume, and repeated rejection.
4. CAG reports, court records, statutory material, and official committee or board records when directly relevant.
5. Method-based institutional research.
6. Reputable reporting that identifies an underlying primary document.

Open the original PDF, annexure, table, or page. A search snippet, media summary, LinkedIn post, commercial guide, or copied article cannot establish an official number.

For every number record:

- exact numerator and denominator;
- claim type or grievance category;
- reporting period;
- unit of analysis, such as claims, members, grievances, or offices;
- whether the number is received, settled, rejected, returned, pending, disposed, or carried forward;
- publication date;
- source URL and page or table number;
- limitation.

Never calculate `claims received minus claims settled = claims rejected`. The difference may contain pending, carried-forward, withdrawn, incomplete, duplicate, or otherwise unresolved claims.

## Counting rules

The internet count is `unique first-person public accounts found`, not total affected EPFO members.

Include a case only when:

- the author says they personally filed the claim or directly handled it for a named relationship;
- the claim state or failure is described with enough detail to classify;
- the item has a visible date within a required window;
- the source URL can be opened and reviewed.

Classify every included item as:

- `DIRECT FORM 19`;
- `PROBABLE FORM 19`, when final settlement is clear but the form number is absent;
- `OTHER EPFO CLAIM`, supporting process evidence only;
- `SECOND-HAND`, excluded from first-person totals;
- `ADVICE OR COMMENTARY`, excluded from first-person totals.

Deduplicate:

- repeated posts or comments by the same account about the same claim;
- cross-posts and syndicated copies;
- quoted or reposted stories;
- the same story discussed on multiple platforms;
- commercial accounts repeating customer examples;
- deleted posts whose details cannot be verified.

When cross-platform identity cannot be established, say so. Report a defensible lower bound instead of inventing a total.

Do not expose UANs, claim IDs, names, Aadhaar or PAN data, phone numbers, bank details, medical details, employer identifiers, or other sensitive information. Assign neutral case IDs.

## Claims that require correction

Do not repeat these earlier claims without current primary evidence:

- `One in three Form 19 claims are rejected.` Aggregate received-versus-settled differences are not automatically rejection rates.
- `Reason Code 15` or any numbered code has a universal official meaning.
- Missing employer attestation is a universal reason for online Aadhaar Form 19 rejection.
- Every technical error should be solved by simply reapplying.
- EPFiGMS has a general 15-to-30-working-day official resolution rule.
- An Aadhaar card being unavailable physically explains why an online Form 19 claim was rejected.
- A blank rejection can be diagnosed from KYC details alone.
- Every `Under Process` claim is delayed. Check the official period and the submission date.

Verify, rather than assume, these current anchors:

- EPFO FAQ statement that a claim is required to be settled within 20 days.
- Citizens' Charter statement that Form 19 has an aspirational seven-working-day target while the scheme limit is 20 days.
- current EPFiGMS registration, document upload, reminder, status, feedback, and reference-number functions;
- current EPFO follow-up path when a grievance remains pending or unsatisfactory;
- whether an official, exhaustive Form 19 rejection-reason catalogue exists.
